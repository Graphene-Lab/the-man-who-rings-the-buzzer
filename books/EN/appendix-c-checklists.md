# Appendix C — Checklists

Print these or keep them on your phone. A checklist is not a cage; it is a net
that catches what a busy mind drops. Tick each item only when it is truly done.

## Pre-visit checklist

Do this before you leave for any round of doors.

- [ ] I know my target list for today and the order I will call on them.
- [ ] For each target, I know the company name, address, and what they do.
- [ ] I have researched each account enough to speak to their situation, not mine.
- [ ] I know the likely pain point for their kind of business.
- [ ] I have a tailored opening line for the most important accounts.
- [ ] I know who I am asking for (the role, if not the name).
- [ ] My 30-second pitch is rehearsed and under thirty seconds.
- [ ] I have my cards, and they are clean and undamaged.
- [ ] I have a one-page useful document to leave where nobody is home.
- [ ] My phone is charged; my CRM/notes app is open and ready to log.
- [ ] I know my goal for each visit (a meeting? a demo? a name?).
- [ ] I know my walk-away position if a price comes up.
- [ ] I am dressed appropriately for the area and the buyer.
- [ ] I have left enough time between visits to write notes, not just drive.

## Day-of-visit checklist

The practical things that decide whether the round goes well.

- [ ] I start with the hardest or most important door first, while my nerve is fresh.
- [ ] At each gate I am calm, polite, and unhurried.
- [ ] I state who I am and who I help in one clear line.
- [ ] I treat every gatekeeper with respect and ask for their name.
- [ ] I ask a clear question (who looks after this?).
- [ ] I listen more than I talk.
- [ ] I leave a useful page where nobody is available.
- [ ] I write down the outcome of every door immediately, not later.
- [ ] I note the gatekeeper's name and any detail for next time.
- [ ] I book a next step (a date) wherever the door is open.
- [ ] I do not take a "no" personally; I move to the next door.
- [ ] At day's end I log every visit in the CRM before I forget.
- [ ] I count: doors rung, people reached, meetings booked.

## Follow-up checklist

The deal is won or lost here more than at the door.

- [ ] Every promised send (quote, document, link) went out on the day I said.
- [ ] Each follow-up email has one clear next step with a date.
- [ ] I mirrored in writing what I said in person, so they hear it twice.
- [ ] My digital footprint (site, reviews) says the same thing my mouth said.
- [ ] No-answer follow-up sent within [2–3] days, light and not pushy.
- [ ] I set a reminder for every open deal so nothing goes cold.
- [ ] I asked for a referral from every happy customer.
- [ ] I updated the CRM with the new status and the next action.
- [ ] I know which deals are stalled and what will unstick them.
- [ ] I stop chasing a clear "no" and leave the door politely open.

## Negotiation-prep checklist

Fill this before you sit down to bargain. A blank cell is your homework.

- [ ] I know my goal (the outcome I want).
- [ ] I know my walk-away point (my floor) and I will not cross it.
- [ ] I know my BATNA — what I do if there is no deal.
- [ ] I have estimated their goal, their ceiling, and their BATNA.
- [ ] I know the ZOPA — the range where a deal is possible.
- [ ] I have my facts and figures ready and correct.
- [ ] I have listed three levers I can trade that cost me little.
- [ ] Every concession I make is traded for something, not gifted.
- [ ] I know who the real decision maker is across the table.
- [ ] I know the culture: how they treat the first number and the word "no".
- [ ] I have a clear close planned (direct, summary, option, or trial).
- [ ] I will end with a date or a decision, never "let me think about it".

## Personal ethics checklist

Run any deal through these. If a line fails, do not cross it.

- [ ] Every claim I made is true and I can prove it if asked.
- [ ] Any scarcity I used is real and I would keep it even if nobody bought.
- [ ] Any social proof I cited is a real customer I am allowed to name.
- [ ] I would say each persuasion line out loud in front of the customer and explain why.
- [ ] I am not using a lever against the customer's own interest.
- [ ] I treated the gatekeeper with full respect and honesty.
- [ ] I used only public, lawful information about the prospect — no intrusion.
- [ ] I did not fake a deadline, a price, or a competitor's offer.
- [ ] If this deal is a bad fit, I said so rather than pushing it through.
- [ ] I would be comfortable if my customer read my notes about them.
- [ ] My reputation would be intact if this customer told the whole town how I sold to them.
