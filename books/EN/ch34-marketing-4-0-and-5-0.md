# Marketing 4.0 and 5.0: The Buzzer in the Digital Age

## Objectives

After this chapter you can:

- Explain Kotler's Marketing 4.0 and 5.0 in plain words and how they differ.
- Combine physical and digital channels around a single buzzer visit.
- Use digital data to personalise a cold approach instead of spraying a generic
  pitch.
- Build one pre-visit brief that fuses web research with your own notes.

## Key concepts

"Marketing 4.0" and "5.0" are labels Philip Kotler gave to two stages of the
digital shift. They matter to the buzzer because they describe the world the
buyer now lives in — a world where a stranger at the door and a search engine on
a phone arrive at the same moment. The seller who understands this stops choosing
between physical and digital and starts using both as one.

### Foundations: Kotler and the digital migration

**Marketing 4.0: Moving from Traditional to Digital** (2017) describes the move
from old channels (TV, print, a one-way pitch) to digital, where the customer
researches, compares, and talks back. Kotler frames the modern customer journey
as the **5A**: Aware, Appeal, Ask, Act, Advocate. A buyer becomes *aware* of you,
feels *appeal*, then *asks* around — reads reviews, searches, messages friends —
before they *act* (buy), and finally *advocate* (recommend you). The key change
is the "Ask" stage: the customer now does their own homework, and you no longer
control the story.

**Marketing 5.0: Technology for Humanity** (2021) goes further. It is about
applying next-generation technology — artificial intelligence, the internet of
things, sensors, and data analytics — in a way that serves people and creates
value for everyone, not just extracts it. Where 4.0 was "go digital," 5.0 is
"use digital wisely, with personalisation and care."

For a seller, the practical lesson is this: the buyer's "Ask" stage happens
before and after you ring the bell. If you show up having done your own asking —
knowing their business, their site, their likely pain — you arrive ahead of their
search. If you show up generic, their phone will know more about you than you
know about them, and you lose.

### Physical-digital channel integration

Kotler calls the blending of physical and digital the **"phygital"** experience.
The buzzer is physical; the buyer's research is digital. The modern visit wraps
the two together.

Think of the visit as three moments, each with a physical and a digital layer:

| Moment | Physical layer | Digital layer |
|---|---|---|
| Before the visit | You walk in | You have researched their site, reviews, news |
| At the door | You speak, you hand a card | The card links to a page, a QR, a booking link |
| After the visit | You leave | A follow-up email, a shared document, a review request |

The mistake is to treat these as separate campaigns. They are one journey. The
card you leave is not decoration; it is a bridge from the physical meeting back
into the digital "Ask" stage the buyer will run that evening. Give them a single
link to a page that answers the questions they are about to search for.

A few concrete integrations:

- Leave a card with a QR code to a one-page summary of exactly what you offer and
  a booking link, so the "Ask" lands on your page, not a competitor's.
- After the visit, send a short email that mirrors what you said in person, with
  one document attached. The buyer hears it twice — once live, once in writing.
- Make sure your own digital footprint (site, reviews, listings) says the same
  thing your mouth said at the door. Contradiction breaks trust.

### Using digital data to personalise

Marketing 5.0's promise is personalisation: using data to make each interaction
fit the specific person. The buzzer has always been personal, but usually on
shaky guesses. Digital data lets you personalise on facts.

Before you ring, gather what is publicly knowable: what the company does, how
big it looks, what they recently launched, what customers praise or complain
about. Then tailor one sentence of your opening to that fact. "I saw you just
opened a second location — new branches usually drown in paperwork before the
first month is out" is a different visit from a generic pitch. It says: I did my
homework, I see your situation, this is for you.

Personalisation is not surveillance. Use only public, lawful information. The
goal is relevance, not intrusion. A buyer welcomes a seller who understands
their business; they resent one who seems to have hacked their inbox. Keep the
line clean: public signals, plainly used.

The danger of going too digital is losing the human edge that made the buzzer
work. Kotler's point is not "replace the human with data." It is "arm the human
with data." The seller who researches and then still shows up in person, calm and
present, combines the reach of digital with the trust of presence. That
combination is what neither a pure online campaign nor a pure cold walk-in can
match.

## Terminology in practice

| Term | Plain meaning | Used in a sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing 4.0 | The shift from traditional to digital marketing | "In Marketing 4.0 the customer researches before you pitch." |
| Marketing 5.0 | Using AI and data to personalise, for humanity | "Marketing 5.0 arms the seller with data, not replaces them." |
| 5A journey | Aware, Appeal, Ask, Act, Advocate | "Their 'Ask' stage happens the night before you ring." |
| Phygital | Blending physical and digital | "The card with a QR link makes the visit phygital." |
| Personalisation | Tailoring to the specific person using data | "Use one researched fact to personalise the opener." |
| Digital footprint | What a company shows online | "Check that your footprint matches your door pitch." |

## A field note

Philip Kotler set out these stages in two books. *Marketing 4.0: Moving from
Traditional to Digital* (2017) describes the customer's shift to digital and the
5A journey (Aware, Appeal, Ask, Act, Advocate), where the buyer's own research
takes control of the story. *Marketing 5.0: Technology for Humanity* (2021)
then applies next-generation technology — AI, the internet of things, sensors,
and data analytics — to marketing in a way meant to serve people and create
value for all stakeholders. The thread between them is the one the buzzer rides:
digital reach, human trust. (Source: Wikipedia, "Philip Kotler.")

## Exercises

1. **Map the 5A for your product.** Write how a typical buyer becomes Aware,
   feels Appeal, Asks around, Acts, and Advocates. Find the one stage where a
   cold visit helps most.
2. **Build a phygital card.** Redesign the card you leave so it carries one link
   or QR that answers the first question the buyer will search that night.
3. **Personalise one opener.** Research a real prospect online and rewrite your
   opening line to include one specific, public fact about them. Read both
   versions aloud and feel the difference.
4. **Write a pre-visit brief.** Before your next visit, gather web facts and your
   own notes into a single one-page brief. If you use AgentBridge, ask it to
   combine the two and read the result on the way over.

## Summary

- Marketing 4.0 is the move to digital, where the buyer researches on their own
  (the 5A journey); Marketing 5.0 is using AI and data to personalise, for
  humanity.
- The buzzer should be phygital: research before, bridge with a link at the door,
  follow up in writing after — one journey, not separate campaigns.
- Use public digital data to personalise one line of your opener; relevance wins,
  intrusion loses.
- Arm the human with data: a researched seller who still shows up in person
  combines digital reach with human trust.
- A single pre-visit brief that fuses web research and your own notes turns a
  cold door into a warm, specific conversation.
