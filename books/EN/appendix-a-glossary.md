# Appendix A — Complete Glossary

Every selling, psychology, negotiation, cultural and marketing term used in this
book, in one A-Z table. Each has a plain one-line definition. If you cannot
explain a word here in a single breath, do not use it at a door.

| Term | Plain definition |
|---|---|
| A/B testing | Comparing two versions (A and B) on similar prospects and keeping the one that wins. |
| Account-based marketing (ABM) | Picking a short list of named target companies first, then tailoring everything to win them. |
| Anchor | The first number put on the table, which frames every number after it. |
| Authority | Influence that comes from shown or documented knowledge, not from claiming it. |
| BATNA | Your best option if you walk away from the deal (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement). |
| Baseline | Your current result, measured before a change, so you can tell if the change helped. |
| Buzzer | The door-to-door seller who rings the intercom in person; also the act of showing up itself. |
| Buyer persona | A profile of your ideal buyer, built from real traits like role, size and problem. |
| Closing | Asking clearly for the agreement; a request, not a trap. |
| Cold call / cold buzzer | Contact with someone who has no prior relationship with you and did not ask to be contacted. |
| Commitment | A small yes that a person then wants to honour, making a bigger yes likelier. |
| Concession | Something you give in a negotiation; it should be traded, never simply gifted. |
| Consistency | Acting in line with what a person has already said they are or want. |
| Conversion rate | The share of attempts that reach the next step (for example, two meetings from twenty doors is 10%). |
| CRM | Software that records every contact and deal (Customer Relationship Management). |
| Cross-selling | Selling an extra, related product alongside the one they want. |
| Decision maker | The person who can say yes and sign. |
| Discovery | The questioning stage where you find the buyer's real needs before pitching. |
| Elevator pitch / 30-second pitch | A very short, ready summary of who you are and the value you bring. |
| Follow-up | The contact you make after a meeting or a quote to keep the deal moving. |
| Funnel | The stages a buyer passes through, wide at the top and narrow at the close. |
| Gatekeeper | The person who controls access to the decision maker, often at reception. |
| Guanxi | In China, the network of trusted relationships that makes business possible. |
| Growth hacking | Fast, low-cost experiments to find what makes a business grow, then scaling what works. |
| High-context culture | A culture where much of the meaning is left unsaid and read from the situation. |
| ICP | The type of company that fits you best (Ideal Customer Profile). |
| Inbound marketing | Drawing buyers to you with useful content instead of chasing them. |
| Key account management (KAM) | Giving your most valuable existing clients special, personal attention. |
| KPI | A number you track to measure performance (Key Performance Indicator). |
| Lead | A person or company that might buy, but is not yet checked or qualified. |
| Lead scoring | Ranking leads by how likely they are to buy, so you call the best ones first. |
| Lever | Something you can trade that the other side values, often cheaper than price. |
| Liking | Warmth and influence from similarity, honest praise and cooperation. |
| Low-context culture | A culture where meaning is stated plainly and directly. |
| Manipulation | A persuasion lever used against the customer's own interest. |
| Nemawashi | In Japan, the quiet behind-the-scenes consensus-building before a decision. |
| Neuromarketing | The study of how the brain responds to marketing and to other people. |
| Nurturing | Steady, useful contact that keeps a not-yet-ready buyer warm over time. |
| Objection | A stated doubt, concern or refusal that you answer before the deal can move. |
| Outbound | Pushing your message out to people who did not ask for it (cold calls, cold buzzers). |
| Pain point | The specific problem that costs the buyer money, time or worry. |
| Persuasion | Helping someone reach a decision by appealing to how they actually think. |
| Pipeline | The full list of your open deals, sorted by stage. |
| Power distance | How much a culture accepts unequal rank and authority. |
| Qualifying | Checking whether a lead has the need, budget and authority to buy. |
| Rapport | A feeling of easy trust and connection between two people. |
| Reciprocity | People feel obliged to return a favour, so giving first can open a door. |
| Referral | A recommendation from an existing contact that introduces you to a new prospect. |
| ROI | What you get back compared to what you spend (Return on Investment). |
| Scarcity | Value that comes from a real, honest limit on supply or time. |
| Sensory marketing | Shaping how a buyer feels through the five senses: voice, touch, sight, space. |
| Social proof | Evidence that people like the buyer have already done it, which makes the choice feel safe. |
| Target account | One specific company you have chosen to pursue. |
| TCO | The full cost of owning something over time, not just the price (Total Cost of Ownership). |
| Threat response | The brain's defence that blocks buying when a person feels rushed or crowded. |
| Trial close | A test question before the final ask, to check for a yes early. |
| Unity | Shared identity — "one of us" — a stronger lever than simple liking. |
| Upselling | Selling a higher or bigger version of what the buyer already wants. |
| USP | What makes you different from every rival (Unique Selling Proposition). |
| Value proposition | The clear promise of benefit you offer the buyer. |
| Warm-up | Making a target familiar to you, and you to them, before you make contact. |
| Win/loss review | Looking back at a closed deal to learn why it was won or lost. |
| Yes ladder | A run of small, easy agreements that makes the real ask feel natural. |
| ZOPA | The range where a deal is possible for both sides (Zone of Possible Agreement). |
