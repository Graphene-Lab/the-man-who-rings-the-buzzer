# Appendix B — Scripts and Templates

Ready-to-use words. Read them, then make them your own. A script is a starting
frame, not a recital — say it plainly, at a calm pace, and listen more than you
talk. Replace every `[bracket]` with your real detail.

## Buzzer / intercom opening script

The goal of the opening is not to sell. It is to get passed through, or to leave
something worth calling back about. Keep it under twenty seconds.

> "Good morning. My name is [your name], from [your company]. I help
> [type of business, e.g. small offices] stop losing hours on [their pain, e.g.
> manual paperwork and invoicing]. Could you point me to whoever looks after
> [the area, e.g. office software], please?"

If nobody is available and you may leave something:

> "No problem at all. I'll leave a one-page note on [a useful fact, e.g. the
> three admin costs most [their kind] firms miss]. My card is with it — my name
> is [your name], and I'll be back in the area on [day]. Who should I ask for?"

Rules for the opening:

- State who you are and who you help in one line.
- Name their pain, not your product.
- Ask a clear, polite question (who looks after this?).
- Never apologise for being there.

## 30-second pitch template

Use when you have the decision maker's ear for half a minute. Four moves.

> **1. Who I help.** "I work with [type of business] who are [situation, e.g.
> growing fast and drowning in admin]."
>
> **2. The problem.** "Right now [the pain, e.g. someone re-types every invoice
> by hand, and it costs hours every week]."
>
> **3. What I do about it.** "I [the value, e.g. set up a system that does the
> paperwork for you and keeps all your data on your own machine]."
>
> **4. Proof and next step.** "[A short proof, e.g. Three firms on your street
> already use it]. Could I show you a two-minute demo, or book fifteen minutes
> this week?"

Keep each move to one sentence. End with a question that asks for a small next
step, never a vague "let me know."

## Gatekeeper-handling script

The gatekeeper is not your enemy. Treat them with respect and honesty; they
decide whether you get in, and they remember.

> "Good morning. I'm [your name] from [your company]. I'm hoping you can help
> me — I don't want to waste anyone's time. Who here looks after [the area]?"

If asked "who told you to call / how did you get this number":

> "I found your business while I was [researching the area / a referral from
> [name, if allowed]]. I'd rather be straight with you: I'm here to see if I can
> help with [the pain]. If it's not useful, I'll be quick."

If they say "send an email":

> "Happy to. So it reaches the right person and isn't junk — who should I
> address it to, and what's the one thing they care most about right now?"

If they block you firmly:

> "Understood, I won't push. Could I leave a short note for [the person], and
> may I ask your name so I mention who was helpful?" (Then leave the note and
> go. Come back another day.)

Never lie to a gatekeeper, never try to sneak past, never be rude. A burned
gatekeeper can shut the whole building.

## Follow-up email templates

### Template 1 — After a good meeting

> **Subject:** Following up on [topic]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Thanks for your time today. As promised, here is [the thing you said you'd
> send: a quote / a short document / the demo link].
>
> To recap what matters to you: [one or two of their stated needs].
>
> Next step: [a clear, dated action — e.g. "I'll call you Thursday to answer
> any questions"]. Does that work?
>
> Best, [your name]

### Template 2 — No reply to a quote

> **Subject:** Any questions on the quote?
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Just checking in on the quote I sent on [date]. No pressure at all — I know
> these things sit.
>
> If it's useful, I'm happy to [walk you through it / adjust the scope /
> answer one question by email]. If the timing's wrong, just say so and I'll
> leave it with you.
>
> Best, [your name]

### Template 3 — Left a note / nobody home

> **Subject:** The one-page note I left you
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I stopped by [day] and left a short note on [the useful topic]. In case it
> didn't reach you, here it is in a line: [the single most useful fact].
>
> I'll be back in the area on [day]. If a five-minute chat would help, reply
> with a time and I'll come by.
>
> Best, [your name]

## Common objections and model responses

Answer the concern behind the words. Never argue. Acknowledge, then reframe.

| Objection | Model response |
|---|---|
| "Not interested." | "Understood. May I ask — is it the timing, or is it not a need at all? That tells me whether to come back or leave it." |
| "Too expensive." | "Compared to what, may I ask? If we look at what [the pain] costs you each month, the price is usually the smaller number. Want me to show you that maths?" |
| "We already have a supplier." | "Good — that means you know the value. I'm not asking you to switch today. Could I show you the one thing we do differently, and you keep it in your pocket?" |
| "Send me an email." | "Will do. So it's actually useful and not junk — what's the one thing you'd want it to answer?" |
| "No budget." | "Makes sense. If there were a way to start small and prove it before any big spend, would that be worth a look — or is it truly a no for this year?" |
| "I need to think about it." | "Of course. So I don't chase you blindly — what's the main thing you want to think over? Often I can settle it now." |
| "Call me in six months." | "I will. What should be different in six months that makes it worth talking then? If nothing changes, shall we pick a smaller step now instead?" |
| "We tried something like this and it failed." | "That's worth hearing. What went wrong? A lot of tools fail for the same two or three reasons — if I know yours, you can see if we avoid them." |

Rule: every response ends with a question that moves the conversation forward or
reveals the real blocker.

## Referral-ask script

Ask for a referral right after a win, when you are at your most likeable.

> "I'm glad this worked out for you. Quick favour — who's one other person you
> know with the same [pain, e.g. paperwork headache]? I'd take good care of
> them the way I've taken care of you. A name, or even a warm introduction by
> email, would mean a lot."

If they hesitate:

> "No pressure. If someone comes to mind later, my card's with you. I only ask
> people I think would genuinely find it useful."

Keep the ask small and specific: one name, not a list. Make it about helping
them, not about your quota.
