# Closing and After-Sales

## Objectives

After this chapter you can:

- Close a deal cleanly and hand it over without dropping the thread.
- Upsell and cross-sell by solving the next real problem, not by pushing.
- Onboard a new customer so they get value in the first weeks.
- Explain **Customer Success Management** and **Value-Based Selling** in plain terms.
- Build a written post-sale plan that turns one sale into many.

## Key concepts

### The buzzer after the sale

Most salespeople treat the signature as the finish line. It is the starting line. The sale you close today is the smallest one you will ever do with this customer — if you handle what comes after. A closed deal that is delivered well becomes repeat business, a bigger contract, and a referral. A closed deal that is dropped becomes a refund, a bad review, and a lost market. The buzzer that got you in the door keeps ringing after the contract is signed.

### Closing techniques that hold

Closing is asking for the decision and then meaning it. The honest closes:

- **The direct close.** "Shall we get started?" Clear, confident, no games.
- **The summary close.** Restate every agreed point, then ask to sign. The list carries them over.
- **The next-step close.** "I'll send the order today and book delivery for the 12th — does that work?" You move into execution, and the yes follows action.

Once they say yes, do not reopen the negotiation. Confirm, document, and move to delivery. A closed deal that keeps getting re-opened in the seller's own head leaks margin and confidence.

### Upselling and cross-selling

**Upselling** is a bigger version of what they bought. **Cross-selling** is a related thing they also need. Both work only when they solve a real next problem. The best time to upsell is when the customer tells you a new need; the worst time is when you need a number this month.

| Move | What it is | When it lands |
|------|-----------|---------------|
| Upsell | A larger tier or longer term of the same thing | When they outgrow the first buy |
| Cross-sell | A related product or service | When a new, adjacent need appears |
| Renewal | Keeping the deal alive | Before the term quietly expires |

Ask a question, not a pitch: "Now that this is running, what's the next thing slowing you down?" Their answer tells you what to offer.

### Customer onboarding

Onboarding is the first ninety days, where the customer decides — often without saying it — whether they made a mistake. A good onboarding has three beats:

1. **Set it up fast.** Get them running with as little friction as possible.
2. **Show early value.** Engineer a small win in week one so the purchase feels right.
3. **Check in.** A short call at day 7 and day 30 catches problems before they become churn.

Most lost customers were not stolen by a competitor. They were never onboarded, so the product never proved itself.

### Customer Success Management and Value-Based Selling

**Customer Success Management (CSM)** is the discipline of owning the customer's outcome after the sale — not just answering tickets, but actively making sure they get the result they bought. A Customer Success Manager watches for the signs of a customer drifting and acts before they leave.

**Value-Based Selling** is selling on the value delivered, not the price of the item. You tie what you sell to a number the customer cares about — money saved, hours returned, risk removed — and you keep proving that number after the sale. When you can show a customer "this saved you 20 hours a month," the renewal and the upsell sell themselves. The invoice stops being a cost and becomes a return.

### Build a post-sale plan

Do not leave after-sales to memory. Write a plan for every new customer:

| When | Action |
|------|--------|
| Day 1 | Kickoff, set them up, confirm what success looks like |
| Day 7 | Check-in call: is it working, any blockers? |
| Day 30 | Value review: show one result they can measure |
| Day 90 | Upsell/cross-sell conversation, and ask for a referral |
| Before renewal | Renewal outreach with the value you delivered |

## Terminology in practice

| Term | Plain meaning | Used in a sentence |
|------|---------------|--------------------|
| Onboarding | Getting a new customer running and seeing value | "We onboard them in week one, not month three." |
| Churn | Customers leaving | "Poor onboarding drives churn." |
| Upsell | A bigger version of what they bought | "They outgrew the starter plan; that's the upsell." |
| Cross-sell | A related product they also need | "The training is a natural cross-sell." |
| CSM | Owning the customer's outcome after the sale | "CSM calls before they drift, not after." |
| Value-Based Selling | Selling on the result, not the price | "We sell the 20 hours saved, not the licence." |

## A field note

"The customer is always right" was not a greeting-card platitude; it was a competitive weapon. Its earliest printed appearance was a *Boston Globe* article in September 1905 describing Chicago merchant Marshall Field. Harry Gordon Selfridge built his London store, Selfridges on Oxford Street (opened 1909), around the idea, and the British press openly mocked the policy as absurd. The hotelier César Ritz's version, *le client n'a jamais tort* — "the customer is never wrong" — was first recorded in 1908. What made it radical was the age: in an era of *caveat emptor*, "let the buyer beware", treating the customer's satisfaction as the rule was a revolution in after-sales. (Source: Wikipedia, "The customer is always right".)

## Exercises

1. **Write a post-sale plan.** Take one real customer and fill the five-row plan above (Day 1, 7, 30, 90, before renewal) with the exact action you will take.
2. **Find the next problem.** Write the one question you will ask a happy customer to uncover an upsell or cross-sell. Practise asking it without turning it into a pitch.
3. **Value number.** For a product you sell, write the single number that proves its value (hours saved, money saved, risk removed). Use it in your next check-in.
4. **Engineer a week-one win.** Design one small result a new customer can see in their first seven days. Write down how you will make it happen.

## Summary

- The signature is the start, not the end; after-sales decides whether the deal repeats.
- Close with a clear ask, then move to execution and stop re-opening the deal.
- Upsell and cross-sell only the real next problem; ask before you offer.
- Onboard fast, show value in week one, and check in before doubt sets in.
- Own the outcome (CSM) and sell on the result (Value-Based Selling) so the invoice reads as a return, not a cost.
