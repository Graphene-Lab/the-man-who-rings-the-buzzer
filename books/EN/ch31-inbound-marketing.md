# Inbound Marketing: The Buzzer Meets the Digital

## Objectives

After this chapter you can:

- Explain inbound marketing and how it differs from outbound door-to-door work.
- Use the buzzer and inbound as partners, not rivals.
- Warm up a cold buzzer with a digital pre-contact on LinkedIn.
- Turn a live agent presence into an inbound asset a prospect can reach on their own.
- Build a simple digital pre-contact sequence before you ring.

## Key concepts

### Foundations: LinkedIn and content in small-business B2B

Inbound marketing is the opposite of interrupting someone. Instead of chasing the
buyer, you make useful things that draw the buyer to you. They search, they read,
they follow, and by the time you speak they already half-know you. In small-business
B2B, the two main inbound channels are content (posts, articles, short videos that
answer real questions) and LinkedIn, where your buyers actually spend their working
day.

The logic is simple. A cold name on a list knows nothing about you. The same name
who has read two of your posts and seen your face on their feed is no longer cold.
You have not changed the person; you have changed what they know before you arrive.
That head start is worth more than any clever opening line.

### The buzzer is not an alternative to inbound

A common mistake is to treat door-to-door and inbound as two camps and pick a side.
That is false. They are the same move at two distances. Inbound warms the ground from
afar; the buzzer closes the distance in person. The strongest sellers run both: they
make enough noise online that when they finally ring, the gate opens faster.

Think of it as a funnel with a face. Inbound pulls many people into a warm pool. The
buzzer is how you personally reach into that pool and turn a warm follower into a
meeting. A buzzer into a cold street is hard. A buzzer into a crowd that already
recognises you is a different sport.

The reverse is also true: every buzzer visit should feed your inbound. The real
conversations you have at the gate tell you what to post about. The questions a
prospect asks you face to face are the exact questions to answer in public content.
The two feed each other.

### Using LinkedIn to warm up the buzzer

Before you ring a target company, spend a week warming it. The steps are small and
repeatable:

1. **Find the person.** Locate your target contact on LinkedIn. Do not message yet.
   Just look: what do they post, what do they care about, what is their company
   working on.
2. **Be visible near them.** Comment thoughtfully on their posts, or on posts their
   industry shares. Add something useful, not "great post." You are becoming a
   familiar face in their feed.
3. **Post something relevant.** Publish a short post that answers a question their
   kind of business has. You are showing, in public, that you understand their
   world.
4. **Then ring.** Now you are not a stranger. "I think we've crossed paths on
   LinkedIn" is a warmer opener than any script.

Warm-up does not guarantee a yes. It removes the wall of total strangerhood, which
is most of the difficulty at a cold gate.

### The agent as an inbound asset

Here is where the two worlds fuse. A live, reachable assistant is itself an inbound
channel. If your capability is always on — reachable by Telegram, by a web chat
page, by phone — a prospect can come to you on their own terms, at midnight, without
waiting for business hours. They experience your responsiveness before they buy
anything.

This matters because responsiveness is a trust signal. A prospect who messages your
Telegram line and gets a clear answer in seconds learns something powerful: this
vendor is fast, always there, and easy to reach. That lesson is learned by doing,
not by your claim. The channel does the selling while you sleep.

## Terminology in practice

| Term | What it means, simply | Used in a real sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Inbound marketing | Drawing buyers to you with useful content instead of chasing them | "We switched to inbound; now prospects arrive already knowing us." |
| Outbound | Pushing your message out to people who did not ask (cold calls, cold buzzers) | "The buzzer is outbound, but a warmed buzzer feels inbound." |
| Warm-up | Making a target familiar to you and you to them before contact | "A week of LinkedIn warm-up turned a cold gate into a hello." |
| Content | Posts or articles that answer a buyer's real questions | "My content answers the exact questions they ask me at the gate." |
| Pre-contact | Any digital touch before the face-to-face meeting | "The pre-contact did the warming; the buzzer did the closing." |
| Always-on channel | A way to reach you that never closes (Telegram, web chat, phone) | "Our always-on Telegram line answers at midnight and sells our responsiveness." |

## AgentBridge in action

Your agent's Telegram presence is a working inbound asset: a live, always-on
demonstration of how fast and reachable you are.

> **A prospect types (in Telegram):**
> `(telegram) How much did we sell yesterday?`
>
> **What it produces:** The agent answers in the Telegram chat in seconds, like a
> colleague who never sleeps. The prospect asked a real question and got a real
> answer instantly, on their own time, without a meeting or a callback.
>
> **Sales angle:** Before you ever ring, let the prospect talk to the thing you
> sell. They message the Telegram line, get an instant, useful reply, and learn by
> doing that you are fast and always there. That is inbound selling with proof: the
> channel warms them up while you are still walking to the office. Pair it with a
> web chat page and you have two front doors that never close.

## A field note

The term "inbound marketing" was coined by Brian Halligan, together with Dharmesh
Shah, around the founding of HubSpot. HubSpot's own account says the two, then MIT
graduate students, noticed buyers shifting away from being interrupted toward
finding answers themselves as early as 2004, and founded the company in 2006 to
build software around that shift. The insight that made the phrase stick is exactly
the one a buzzer seller can borrow: stop chasing people who did not ask, and build
things that make them come to you already warm.

## Exercises

1. **Warm one gate for a week.** Pick one target company. For five days, find the
   contact on LinkedIn, leave one genuinely useful comment, and publish one short
   post that answers a question their business has. On day six, ring and note how
   different the opening feels.
2. **Write your pre-contact sequence.** On paper, map the digital touches you will
   make before a buzzer visit: which channel, what you post or send, in what order.
   Keep it to three steps you can repeat for every target.
3. **Open a front door.** Set up one always-on way for a prospect to reach your
   capability directly — a Telegram line or a web chat page. Send three friendly
   prospects there with no pressure and watch what they ask.
4. **Harvest your questions.** After your next three buzzer visits, write down the
   three questions prospects asked you. Turn each into a short public post. You are
   now feeding inbound from outbound.

## Summary

- Inbound draws buyers with useful content; outbound (the buzzer) chases them. You
  need both.
- A warmed target is not a stranger, and that removes most of the difficulty at a
  cold gate.
- Warm up on LinkedIn before you ring: be visible, be useful, then show up.
- A live, always-on agent (Telegram, web chat) is an inbound asset that sells your
  responsiveness by being reached, not by being described.
- Feed the loop: the questions you hear at the gate become the content that warms
  the next crowd.
