# Sales Strategies in the United States: Speed, Clarity, and Consensus

## Objectives

After this chapter you can:

- Treat speed as a selling value in the US, not as rudeness.
- Sell as a trusted advisor instead of a pitchman.
- Name and fight the real enemy: "no decision."
- Map and move an internal buying committee.
- Build a 30-second pitch tuned for an American buyer.

## Key concepts

The United States is the fastest large buying market in the world. Americans buy
quickly, expect you to be quick, and punish slowness. But speed alone is not a
strategy. The American buyer also wants clarity — know exactly what you offer and
what it is worth — and a path through a committee that can quietly kill a deal.
Win the speed, win the clarity, and unblock the committee, and the US opens wide.

### Speed as a value

In the US, time is money in the most literal sense. A buyer reads your speed as a
signal of your competence. Answer in hours, not days. Get to the point in the
first sentence. Follow up the same day. A slow reply is read as a weak offer or a
distracted vendor.

This does not mean rushing the close. It means removing dead time. The American
"no" is often just a slow "maybe" that died of neglect. Speed keeps the deal alive.
Three habits:

- Reply fast, even if the answer is "I will send the numbers by three."
- Lead with the payoff. "This saves you four hours a week" before any background.
- Always propose the next step and a time. Never leave the meeting without a date.

### Consultative selling and the trusted advisor

The most respected seller in America is not the loudest; it is the advisor.
Consultative selling means you lead with the buyer's problem and a point of view
about how to solve it, not with your product. You behave like a doctor: diagnose
before you prescribe.

The trusted advisor is the highest form of this. The idea comes from the 2000 book
*The Trusted Advisor* by David H. Maister, Charles H. Green, and Robert M.
Galford. They put trust in a simple formula, the Trust Equation:

> Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-orientation

Read it as a fraction. Credibility is "do they know their stuff." Reliability is
"do they do what they say." Intimacy is "do I feel safe with them." All three sit
on top. Self-orientation — how much the seller is clearly in it for themselves —
sits underneath, and it divides everything. A seller who seems self-interested
shrinks trust no matter how smart or reliable they are. The lesson for the buzzer:
be visibly on the buyer's side. Ask about their business before you talk about
yours. The advice that costs you something to give is the advice they believe.

### The problem of "no decision"

In complex B2B selling the biggest competitor is not a rival firm. It is the
buyer doing nothing. Status quo wins more deals than any competitor. Researchers
call this "no decision" — the deal that never closes because nobody felt enough
urgency to change.

"No decision" happens when three things are missing:

- **Pain is not sharp enough.** The problem is annoying, not expensive. Make the
  cost concrete: hours lost, money leaking, risk carried.
- **No owner.** Nobody inside the company is accountable for fixing it. Find the
  person who feels the pain and give them a reason to own it.
- **No forcing event.** Nothing makes the change urgent. Tie your offer to a date
  the buyer already cares about — a budget cycle, a contract renewal, a launch.

Your job is to make "doing nothing" more expensive than "doing something."

### The role of internal consensus

A US purchase rarely has one decider. It has a buying committee: the user, the
manager, finance, IT, procurement, sometimes legal. Each has a veto. You are not
selling to a person; you are selling to a group that must agree.

Map the committee before you go deep. For each seat, know three things: their
role, what they care about, and whether they help or block.

| Seat | Cares about | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| User | Does it make my day easier | Show the workflow, get a champion |
| Manager | Results, team output | Tie to a metric they own |
| Finance | Cost, payback, risk | Give the ROI number and the payback period |
| IT / Security | Safety, integration | Answer the data and access questions early |
| Procurement | Price, terms, paper | Be easy to buy from, have the docs ready |

Sell the champion first — the person who wants the change — then arm them to sell
the room for you when you are not there. Give them the one-page summary, the ROI
figure, the answer to the security question. A good champion with good ammunition
closes the committee in the hallway.

### Applying the buzzer in the USA

The buzzer in America is fast, direct, and benefit-led. Ring, state who you are
and what you do for them in one line, and ask for the next step. Do not warm up
for five minutes; Americans read a long warm-up as evasion. Be confident but not
arrogant — confidence opens the door, arrogance closes it.

A working American opening:

> "Hi, I'm [name]. We help companies like yours cut [specific cost] by
> [specific amount]. Can I take thirty seconds to show you how, and fifteen
> minutes with whoever owns [the area]?"

Notice the shape: speed, a number, a small clear ask. Then, inside the meeting,
switch from pitchman to advisor: ask questions, diagnose, and let the buyer reach
your conclusion with you. Speed gets you in the room; the trusted-advisor stance
keeps you there; the committee map gets you the signature.

## Terminology in practice

| Term | Plain meaning | Used in a sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Consultative selling | Selling by advising on the buyer's problem | "He consultative-sold: questions first, product second." |
| Trusted advisor | A seller trusted like a personal expert | "She became their trusted advisor, not just a vendor." |
| Self-orientation | How much a seller seems in it for themselves | "High self-orientation divides his trust down to nothing." |
| No decision | A deal lost to the status quo, not a rival | "We lost to no decision, not to a competitor." |
| Buying committee | The group that must agree to buy | "Finance was the silent seat on the buying committee." |
| Champion | An insider who sells for you | "Her champion pushed the deal through the committee." |

## A field note

The "trusted advisor" is a modern name for an old truth, but it was given a
formula that makes it usable. In *The Trusted Advisor* (Free Press / Simon &
Schuster, 2000; 20th-anniversary Touchstone edition 2021), David H. Maister,
Charles H. Green, and Robert M. Galford wrote trust as a fraction: Trust =
(Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-orientation. The structure is the
lesson. The three good qualities add; the one bad quality — being visibly in it
for yourself — divides. A brilliant, reliable seller who smells self-interested
can score lower on trust than a modest one who seems to care. For the man at the
buzzer, the arithmetic is a behaviour rule: lower the denominator before you
raise the numerator. (Source: Maister, Green & Galford, *The Trusted Advisor*,
2000.)

## Exercises

1. **The 30-second US pitch.** Write your opening for an American buyer: one
   line of who you are, one line of the specific cost you cut and by how much,
   one small ask with a time. Read it aloud; it must land in thirty seconds.
2. **Lower the denominator.** Take your pitch and mark every line that is about
   you. Rewrite each to be about the buyer. Count how many "we/our" become
   "you/your."
3. **Kill no decision.** For one real target, write the three things that make
   doing nothing expensive: the sharp pain, the owner who feels it, and the
   forcing date. If you cannot name all three, the deal will die of neglect.
4. **Map the committee.** List the five seats at a target company. For each,
   write what they care about and the one line you would give your champion to
   sell that seat for you.

## Summary

- Speed is a value in the US: reply fast, lead with the payoff, always set a date.
- Sell as a trusted advisor; lower self-orientation before you raise credibility.
- The real enemy is "no decision" — make the status quo more expensive than change.
- A US deal is a committee; find the champion and arm them to sell the room.
- AgentBridge keeps your materials at American speed: a deck per buyer plus a
  researched comparison, built before you ring.
